# 6. The print sheet (PRINT.md)

**You do:** follow `PRINT.md` at the printer and the workbench.
**The agent does:** writes `PRINT.md` so you don't need the chat to print and assemble.

## What is in it

- **Files**: which STL to print, how many, and which way up (already set in
  the file).
- **Slicer notes**: layer height, supports (usually none), brims, and
  anything to look at in the preview.
- **Other parts**: magnets, screws, glue, with sizes and counts.
- **Assembly steps**: one action per step, in order, with a dry fit before
  any glue.
- **What to report back** afterwards.

## Using it

1. Open each STL in your slicer. Do not rotate it unless the sheet says so.
2. Check the slicer's preview: scroll through the layers and look for parts
   floating in the air, or thin walls that disappeared. If something looks
   wrong, ask the agent before printing.
3. Print. Note anything odd: lifting corners, stringing, a failed bridge.
4. Assemble in the order given. **Dry-fit before gluing.** If a step does not
   make sense or a part does not fit, stop and tell the agent; don't force it.

## Safety notes

- Superglue (CA) bonds skin in seconds. Use a toothpick to apply it.
- Small magnets are dangerous if swallowed; keep them away from children
  and pets.

## In the example

Read the keypad's [PRINT.md](../examples/keypad-sculpture/PRINT.md). Look at
the magnet trick: stack all the magnets, mark the top of each with a dot,
and every dot is then the same pole. A check before the glue sets catches a
flipped magnet.
